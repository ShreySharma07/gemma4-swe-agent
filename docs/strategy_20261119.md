# Strategy to Dec 2 (Nov 19): spend less on thinking, buy calls back, and go for the small-model prize

This note builds on everything in `docs/experiments.md`, `docs/HQ.md` and `docs/findings.md` up to Nov 19. It is a recommendation; nothing here is decided until Shrey says so.

**Time left:** 13 days. **Leaderboard slots left:** about 12. The rules allow one submission per UTC day with only one pending at a time, and each run takes about 24 h to score. So a slot opens only when the previous run scores, and a timeout costs a day. **GPU:** about 120 quota-h across both accounts until Dec 2 (resets Nov 21 and Nov 28). GPU hours are not the constraint. The constraint is the number of slots and the days on the calendar.

## 1. What 27 submissions tell us

| Kind of change | Runs | LB |
|---|---|---|
| **Removed a broken thing** | v2 (−`read_file`), v7 (−`edit_file`) | 0.00 → 0.08 → 0.15 |
| **Thinking on + public budgets / public recipe** | v14, v17 (×2), v23 | 0.17, 0.18, 0.18, 0.18 |
| **Added work or instructions** | v9 (70 calls), v10b (test rule), v13, v15b (spec prompt), v21 (read-first) | 0.12, 0.10, 0.10, 0.08, 0.13 |
| **Output reservation cut** | v11 (4096), v15 (6144) | 0.10, crash |

| **Smaller model** | v26 (E4B) | 0.06 |

**Lesson 1: only changes that removed something moved the LB.** Every change that added instructions, exploration or calls scored at or below its base. The hidden tasks punish extra work.

**Lesson 2: three limits stop the agent, and the same thing drives all three.**
- **Time.** The 12 h cap allows a margin of under about 10%. v18 broke it with +11% decode and v22 with +43% calls.
- **Context.** The usable prompt is 24.5k tokens. 13–30% of tasks overflow (v14 and v17 on new-60), and 0% of those resolve.
- **Calls.** 44% of failures end at the 28-call cap, and 80% of those had already seen the gold lines.

Generated tokens feed all three. About 86% of task time is decode, and generated tokens are 43–48% of the context's growth. Most long thinking steps are repetition loops: in v17, 25 of 33 steps of 2k tokens or more repeat themselves. Tasks with such a step resolve 25%; tasks without one resolve 62%.

**Every wasted thinking token costs time, context and, indirectly, calls.** This is the one lever that loosens all three limits at once. We have probed it only from the side (v24, v25, v27).

**Lesson 3: the leaderboard is the only instrument we can trust, and it is coarse.**
- Local sets do not predict the LB. Every local set we have is public code, which the model has likely memorised. v14 resolves 47% of F3 real bugs locally but scores 0.17 on the LB.
- One LB run separates two variants only when they differ by 0.04–0.05 or more.
- With 12 readings left, we can detect only **large** effects. Small prompt tweaks cannot be told apart from noise.

**Lesson 4: self-training the 31B is used up.**
- Three adapters (T1, T2a, T2b) cut validation loss by about 40% and changed nothing on the LB.
- Each costs 11–15% of wall time, more than the time margin we have.
- The published gains from SFT on agent trajectories (SWE-Gym, SWE-smith) come from a **stronger teacher**. Our 31B never had one, and the teacher API route failed (TP1).

**Lesson 5: the 0.24 group is mostly lucky draws of our own recipe family.**
- Every public 0.22–0.24 recipe we have read is v17's family: temperature 0.2, thinking 4096, 28–40 calls.
- Public census: the same recipe scores 0.13–0.18 across 10 draws, mean about 0.166.
- Our v17 family scored 0.18 on each of three runs, which is at or above the field's mean.
- If the private board scores tasks that are not on the public board, best-of-N public scores will fall back toward their means. Our private rank is then probably better than our public rank.
- To **win**, though, we need a real gain of about +0.04–0.06 in the mean, not another draw.

**Hypothesis worth knowing (not verified): the scorer may give the same score to byte-identical submissions.** With tasks run one at a time and a seeded vLLM, the random stream repeats.
- For: v17 and N1 (byte-identical) both scored exactly 0.18, and 14 teams sit at exactly 0.24.
- Against: one public claim of an identical resubmission scoring 0.20 and then 0.15.
- If true, N1 was not an independent draw, our noise estimate is optimistic, and repeating a byte-identical file wastes a slot.
- **Rule:** any repeat we submit gets a trivial change (for example one prompt word), so it counts as a fresh draw.

**Open fact to check (Shrey, 2 min):** the competition's Evaluation tab.
- Our notes say about 120 hidden tasks, split 50/50 between the public and private boards. Yet every score seen is a multiple of 1/120, not 1/60.
- Which tasks the private board scores decides how finals should be picked.

## 4. What we stop doing
- Prompt-rule variants on the 31B. Five tries; none beat the base.
- 31B adapters, the reviewer/multi-agent designs, graph tools and skills, the teacher API route.
- Byte-identical resubmissions (possibly not independent draws; see §1).
- Decision rules on intermediate mechanism measures. v27's rule dropped a variant whose outcomes (time, overflow, submits, resolves) all improved, because one loop measure landed at 0.20–0.24 against a 0.2 bar. From now on we keep pre-registering, but the rule is written on resolves, runtime and overflow, and mechanism numbers are reported, not gated on.

## 5. Risks
- **Timeout = a lost day.** Every submission that changes time per task (A3, B1, B5) gets a concurrency-1 timing run first. Projected runtime must be ≤ v17's for the 31B and ≤ 9 h for E4B.
- **E4B LoRA may not be servable** (Gemma 4 E4B architecture under vLLM 0.19.1 LoRA). B0 settles this before any trainer work is spent.
- **Temperature 1.0 raises malformed calls** (v27: one task looped on a `write_file` call missing its path). If A1 regresses, the fallback is temperature 0.6, not more prompt rules.
- **Distillation may not transfer** (synthetic factory issues; E4B may imitate the 31B's long thinking). Mitigations: loop filter, the real-bug F3 gate, new-60 data mixed in.
