# Open gaps: code-graph comprehension × LLM graph reasoning (survey of Oct 22, 2026)

Four parallel surveys feed this document. Their full notes, with paper tables (ID, method, key number, limitation), are in `surveys_2026-10-22/`:
- **A:** code-graph construction, parsing and embedding (~52 papers).
- **B:** code graphs inside SWE agents (~40 papers).
- **C:** LLM reasoning over large graphs (~70 papers).
- **D:** citation audit of our earlier notes (59 works).

Every paper below was found through alphaXiv, arXiv or web search. Numbers taken only from abstracts, or not confirmed, are marked as such in the survey files.

**Headline.** Structure gives modest accuracy gains. Adding a code graph to a strong agent buys +1–3 pp:
- RepoGraph: +2.3 pp.
- CodeAnchor: +1–2 pp Func@5.
- CodeNib: one-hop expansion is not significant.

The reliable wins are efficiency and stability:
- 25–87% fewer tokens.
- About half the run-to-run variance.

Almost all evidence comes from memorised public repos. On the graph-reasoning side, both of the following beat explicit structure once you control for them:
- lexical global ranking plus selective access;
- good evidence handling.

Most published "graph" gains are not shown to come from topology.

Tag legend:
- **[new]**: not in our earlier notes.
- **[ext]**: extends an earlier gap.
- **[fix]**: corrects an earlier claim.

## 1. Code comprehension (code graphs, parsing, embedding, use by agents)

**CC1. The structure × familiarity confound has never been crossed. [ext]**

Every graph-for-agents paper runs on SWE-bench-family repos (12–40 public repos):
- RepoGraph 2410.14684
- LocAgent 2503.09089
- CGM 2505.16901
- RPG-Encoder 2602.02084
- ARISE 2605.03117
- CodeAnchor 2606.26979
- SeeRepo/DualView 2606.14061
- LARGER 2605.16352

Models already know these repos:
- SWE-Bench Illusion (2506.12286): up to 76% file recall from the issue text alone.
- Code-QA-Bench (2605.29277): closed-book score ≈0.5.
- SchrödingerRepo (2609.27891): renaming costs 6–14% pass@1, with 82% of the extra actions spent on exploration, and causes no drop on post-cutoff tasks.

Exploration is exactly the phase that graph tools accelerate. So the +1–3 pp is plausibly a *lower* bound on unseen code. The opposite is also plausible: graph lookups may fail more once names lose meaning.

**Missing experiment:** {graph on/off} × {original / renamed / unseen repo}, run with a small open model, paired McNemar.

**CC2. Structure under a hard context/step budget, with weak tool-callers. [ext]**

Efficiency gains (25–87% tokens) are measured with frontier models on generous budgets, for example 250 steps or $3 per task. Nobody converts them into accuracy under a hard cap like 32k context with no compaction.

The delivery channel matters, but has only been compared with frontier models:
- LARGER puts neighbours inside the agent's own grep output: File Acc@5 rises from 74 to 87.
- CodeAnchor injects edges as source comments, using `called_by` links.
- RepoGraph: a flattened 2-hop subgraph *hurts* (26.0 vs 27.3).
- RepoNavigator (2512.20957): adding tools lowers IoU from 24.3 to 13.7.

Passive, grep-visible structure is untested on open models of 30B or less.

**CC3. Name resolution is the unmeasured failure of graph lookups. [new]**

Graph tools keyed on fully qualified names need fuzzy fallbacks:
- LocAgent's BM25 fallback is its largest ablation.
- Codebase-Memory uses a 6-tier fuzzy cascade.
- LARGER frames localization as "lexical match → structural entry".

No paper reports the fraction of graph calls that return nothing because the agent passed a phrase or a bare name. Our v5 data (0 results for bare names) is a direct instance.

**CC4. Graph correctness: dynamic-language edge errors and in-trajectory staleness. [new]**
- CodeNib (2607.25431): static references agree with live LSP only 39% of the time. Incremental repair matches a full rebuild in only 45.5% of transitions.
- Python call graphs are heuristic (PyCG, CHA, fuzzy tiers).
- No study injects false-positive vs false-negative edges against a runtime-trace ground truth.
- No issue-resolution paper measures stale edges after the agent's own edits; every paper builds the graph once on the base commit.

**CC5. No on-demand inter-procedural data-flow or slicing tool for Python agents. [new]**
- ARISE's *intra*-procedural slice gives the largest controlled gain: +7 pp R@1, and the empty-slice control shows the effect is real. Its residual failures have their root cause in a callee.
- CodeAnchor's eager data-flow tags saturate.
- Query-time inter-procedural slicing is the obvious next interface, and it is unexplored.

**CC6. Structure-aware training that ships as an adapter, and the matched-compute comparison of graph tools vs bash. [ext]**
- CGM needs a custom encoder and an attention mask, so it cannot run on PEFT/vLLM.
- Function-aware FIM mid-training (2607.12463) gives +2.8–5.3 pp on Verified, but as full-weight mid-training on 2.6B tokens.
- Graph-tool RL (ToolTrain, RepoNavigator) and bash RL (CodeScout 2603.17829) each train one interface, with localization rewards only.

Nobody has:
- trained two identical models that differ only in having graph tools, and measured resolve rate;
- used a reward for traversing the gold call path;
- done any of this as a LoRA on a quantized base.

**CC7. Localization handoff when the localizer and the fixer are the same small model. [new]**

Every positive handoff result uses a localizer stronger than the fixer. With a 30B model in both roles, SHERLOC (2606.24820, Table 16) shows findings that are neutral or harmful: 49.4 → 42.2.

From Finding to Fixing (2610.25826, 16k trajectories):
- Fault localization (FL) barely helps strong agents.
- Its value doubles when location clues are removed from the issue.
- A cause description is worth more than locations.
- Agents anchor on wrong FL.

What has not been crossed: granularity × diagnostic fields × fixer size, with real (imperfect) localizers and a confidence gate.

**CC8. No benchmark is unseen, structurally deep and keyword-free together. [new]**
- Over 50% of localization issues name the gold location. The only keyword-free set has 25 queries.
- RepoProbe (2608.04783) uses post-2024 repos, but they are public and its depth is uncontrolled.
- LocAgent shows accuracy falls with hop distance, but SWE-bench hops are mostly 1–2.

Missing: held-out or private repos with questions or issues that need ≥2-hop call or data-flow reasoning that grep can't short-circuit.

**CC9. Code embeddings and graph expansion: contradictory results and no structural invariance. [new]**
- SpIDER: call-edge expansion gives +27% Recall@20.
- CodeNib: one-hop expansion's confidence interval includes zero.
- Nobody has reconciled these two through seed quality, edge type or benchmark.
- Code embedders (C2LLM, jina-code) are trained text-only.
- CGBridge (2512.07666) shows a GNN soft prompt is nearly rename-invariant (−1.6% vs −15 to −28% for text baselines), but only on snippets.
- Neither graph-neighbour contrastive embeddings nor repo-level graph-token models (CGM) have been tested under renaming.

**CC10. Repository memory without history. [new]**

RepoMem (2510.01003) gains scale with commit-history depth, and the method loses 13 points on sparse-history repos. In the offline snapshot setting (no git log, no issues), the remaining memory sources are untested:
- links between tests and code;
- docstrings;
- experience carried across tasks within a run.

## 2. LLM reasoning over large graphs

**GR1. No deep-reasoning evaluation on graphs with 10^5–10^7 nodes. [new]**
- Graph-in-context work stops at about 100 nodes: G1 (2505.18499), GT-Bench (2609.12265).
- KGQA over large knowledge graphs uses 1–4 hops from given (oracle) topic entities, as in Search-on-Graph-R1 (2607.18481).
- GraphChain (2511.00457) reaches 79.5% at 200k nodes on its own benchmark.

Nobody combines a huge graph with deep or global queries and verifiable answers.

**GR2. RL has not been shown to go beyond SFT. [new]**
- Shortest-path study (2604.15306, ICLR'26): RL ≤ best SFT.
- SoG-R1: SFT adds +50 points, RL only +1–2.
- Process rewards help on synthetic tasks, not real ones (2506.00845).
- Fine-tuned G1-style models become *more* sensitive to format and structure (Lost in Serialization 2511.10234).

All transfer tests share a distribution with training.

**GR3. Size and length generalization: there is theory, but no recipe for pretrained LLMs. [new]**
- Connectivity capacity is diameter ≤ 3^L. Training beyond capacity teaches a node-degree heuristic (2510.19753).
- Search needs log depth and width (2405.18512).
- About 1% slightly-longer training data rescues length generalization (2604.15306).

All of this is shown only on small transformers trained from scratch, never on pretrained LLMs doing tool-mediated search.

**GR4. No accepted test that a model actually uses topology. [new]**
- When Graph Tokens Sink (2606.03712): the salient graph tokens are padding, and removing them changes nothing.
- GTEval (2605.03514): models rely on the text.
- Cheap baselines come within 2–3 points of reported GraphLLM results, and evaluation leaks exist (2610.28714).
- The Thousand-Graph Hypothesis (2608.26602) argues explicit edges are unnecessary, on weak evidence.

Shuffle and relabel controls are not standard practice.

**GR5. Invariance to encoding and renaming needs ground-truth graphs. [ext]**
- The best input format varies with model × task × density (2609.12265).
- GRAIN (2608.27142) halves the OOD gap with a structure-invariance reward, but the reward needs the gold topology and the tests use synthetic undirected graphs only.

**GR6. GraphRAG is conditional and unpriced at scale. [new]**
- BM25 beats every graph index above about 10M tokens, and LightRAG's index build fails at 2,826 docs (2607.26497).
- GraphRAG hurts on simple facts and on math (2506.05690, 2506.02404).
- Agentic search after a global BM25 ranking scores 69.4%. An agent browsing raw files scores about 32 points lower.

**GR7. Evidence management may be the real bottleneck. [ext]**
- GRA (2608.15834): a graph-free control shows the gain comes from selective access, not from topology.
- Wikidata Traces (2610.06650): long-horizon failures are evidence-management failures.
- GraphEcho (2609.17695): agents re-walk redundant paths.

No benchmark separates graph-tool quality from context handling under a fixed token budget.

**GR8. Step-level path verification is missing outside KGQA. [new]**
- GCR/KG-Trie gives faithfulness by construction, but only for paths that fit in a trie.
- KG-Guard and conformal methods verify answers, not paths.
- No cheap edge-existence checker is used as an RL reward for agentic exploration.

**GR9. Latency and cost are rarely reported. [new]**

KGCache: 1.91× faster retrieval gives only a 1.06× end-to-end speedup, because LLM calls dominate. Wall-clock under a fixed budget is almost never measured.

## 3. Where the two fields meet (the most promising, least crowded area)

**X1. Code is a free source of ground-truth graphs for graph-reasoning training.**

GR5 and GR8 are blocked because rewards need the true topology. Repositories supply one cheaply, through static analysis plus runtime traces. Two things are possible:
- GRAIN-style structure rewards ("does the call chain the agent claims exist?");
- KG-Trie-style constrained symbol names.

Both are possible on code, and nobody has done either. The catch is CC4: static graphs are imprecise, so the reward has to tolerate edge noise. That is itself an open question.

**X2. Topology-use controls have never been applied to code graphs.**

Code-graph papers rarely run GR4-style controls (shuffled edges, relabelled nodes, empty tool output). There are only two exceptions:
- ARISE's empty-slice control;
- SHERLOC's shuffled findings (−4.5 pp).

Combining these with renaming (CC1) would separate three things:
- memorised names;
- lexical anchoring;
- actual use of structure.

**X3. Global lexical ranking first, then local structure, as an interface for small models.**

Three independent results converge:
- BM25-at-scale (GR6);
- LARGER's lexical-to-structural anchoring;
- SoG-R1's single batched one-hop tool.

Together they point to "rank globally, then expand a bounded neighbourhood". This has not been compared head to head for code, with a small model under a hard cap, and the SpIDER vs CodeNib contradiction (CC9) shows the outcome is not obvious.

**X4. Evidence-hit vs conversion has not been decomposed for SWE agents.**

GraphDancer (2602.02518) and Wikidata Traces split failures into two kinds:
- "never saw the evidence";
- "saw it and didn't use it".

From Finding to Fixing's anchoring results suggest the second kind is large in SWE agents, but no paper measures it from trajectories.

**X5. Size generalization for repository navigation.**

Repository graphs are orders of magnitude larger than any training graph. Two results make depth the variable to study:
- the GR3 theory (diameter capacity, heuristic collapse);
- LocAgent's accuracy falling with hop distance.

Diameter-bounded curricula, or slightly-longer augmentation for multi-hop localization trajectories, are untested.

## 5. The gaps that fit our setting best

This is a short ranking, not a plan.

**1. CC1 + X2.** This is the paper-track thesis candidate. The design is graph/structure on vs off × original vs renamed repos, using Gemma-4-31B-QAT, paired McNemar. It directly tests whether "local gains don't transfer to private repos" comes from memorization.

**2. CC2 + CC3 + X3.** These are submission-relevant:
- grep-visible, comment-injected or LARGER-style structure needs no new tool and no fully qualified name;
- the 32k no-compaction cap is exactly the untested regime.

**3. CC7.** Our localizer and fixer are the same small model, and the evidence leans negative. This argues for checking that our localizer sub-agent pays for its context before investing in it further.
