## 1. Literature Taxonomy & Overview

The intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs), graph reasoning, and autonomous software engineering (SWE) agents has moved through three broad phases from 2023 to late 2026:

1. **Flat text and heuristic RAG (2023–2024).** Repositories were treated as unstructured text chunks, indexed with BM25, TF-IDF or dense bi-encoder embeddings (e.g., SWE-agent, early Agentless).

2. **Explicit code graphs with traversal tools (2024–2025).** Repositories were parsed into heterogeneous graphs (containment, imports, calls, inheritance), and LLMs got graph tools: RepoGraph, LocAgent, CodexGraph, RANGER.

3. **Robustness, evaluation and evidence quality (2025–2026).** Three strands make up this phase:
   - Studies showing that agents rely on memorised or lexical cues rather than structure (Schrödinger's Code Repository, Jagged Frontier, Lexical Superstition).
   - Diagnostic benchmarks for repository reasoning (RepoProbe, RepoReason, SWE-QA-Pro).
   - Work outside code on whether graph connectivity actually supports an answer (PAGE-RAG, PAGR, GraphWalk).

   Over-squashing at hub modules (Alon & Yahav; Topping et al.) is *our hypothesis* for why repository graphs are hard to propagate over. No cited paper measures it on code graphs.

## 3. Methodological & Synthesis Summary

Across the 32 reviewed studies, four conclusions emerge. Each is marked as either supported by the cited papers or our own hypothesis.

1. **The graph delivery dilemma (partly supported).**
   - Passive, grep-visible structure gives small but significant localization gains and lower variance (#28: +2.2 pp Func@5).
   - Structure delivered as output can also hurt when it is too large (#1: a flattened 2-hop subgraph scores below baseline).
   - No paper here directly compares optional graph tools with passive injection.
   - Our own evidence: in our Gemma 4 harness, `search_similar_code` was called 0 times in 461 tool calls, and it returns nothing for phrases or bare names because it is keyed by qualified name.

2. **The lexical shortcut (supported).**
   - Agents rely on familiar names and lexical cues (#16, #17, #18).
   - Under renaming and remapping, Pass@1 drops 6.0–14.4 pp, and 81.6–83.6% of the extra actions go to exploration (#16).
   - The drop disappears on post-cutoff tasks (#16), so it reflects memorization.
   - Fine-tuning does not remove lexical over-reliance in code-comprehension models (#18). This has not been tested for SWE agents.

3. **Premature commitment (partly supported).**
   - In graph navigation, about half of the residual errors of a trained 3B agent are premature stops (#27).
   - Our own Step 2 gate showed the analogous pattern: the S3 adapter submitted after a test failure 28 of 32 times (87.5%).
   - The earlier attribution of this pattern to SWE-smith and "TraceLift" was removed. Neither claim was verified.

4. **Graph bottlenecks and over-squashing (hypothesis).**
   - Message passing over large graphs suffers over-squashing (#24, #25).
   - That repository hub modules are negatively curved, and that this explains agent localization failures, is *our hypothesis*. It has not been measured.
   - Separately, graph proximity does not imply evidential support (#7).

These conclusions, with their stated level of support, form the starting point for our proposed research.
