# General (non-code) LLM reasoning over large graphs: methods, scaling, process supervision, failure modes, and what transfers to code graphs (as of 8 Oct 2026)

Scope note: this covers general graph/KG/GraphRAG work. The papers the project already covers (GRAIN, GraphDancer, Agentic Graph Tokens, When Graph Tokens Sink, G1, GraphWiz, Graph-R1, GraphPO, AgentGL) are referred to only for contrast. Labels used: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed venue confirmed; **[pre]** = arXiv preprint (no venue found); **VERIFIED** = I read the claim in the paper text or its alphaXiv report during this session; **INFERRED** = my reasoning or recall, not checked against a source this session. Target setting throughout: fixed Gemma-4-31B (QAT), 32k context, LoRA only, call graphs of ~1k–5k nodes / 2k–6k edges (undirected in practice, one mixed "calls" label), navigated through tool calls under a tight call budget (~4.5–5 min per task, harness cap 100 calls).

## Q1. Which approaches scale to graphs far bigger than the context, and what do they cost in calls/tokens?

### Takeaway
Serialising the whole graph does not fit a 2k–6k-edge call graph into 32k tokens, and even when it fits, linearised structure causes attention shortcuts. What scales is (a) a global, cheap candidate-discovery index (BM25/embeddings) followed by (b) agentic local expansion with batched neighbour tools. LLM-built GraphRAG indices (community summaries, triple extraction) hit "construction walls" and lose to BM25 at scale. The strongest 2026 evidence says the gain from agentic graph access comes mostly from *selective access*, not from topology itself, and it only appears when the model calls tools reliably. Typical costs: ~2–5 tool calls for trained KG agents on KGQA, ~11–15 calls for generic graph agents, and an accuracy knee at ~30 calls.

### Inferences
- For a 31B model at 32k context, the scalable recipe is: **global candidate ranker (BM25 over symbol names/docstrings + the provided embeddings) → passive compact map → a few batched, direction-typed neighbour calls**. This combines the BM25-at-scale and SOG-R1 findings. INFERRED.
- GRA's knee at ~30 calls is for 11–15-turn QA. Our per-task budget must also cover reading code, editing and testing, so the graph-navigation slice is probably ~5–10 calls. That is below the knee, so per-call information density (batched queries, paging, direction filters) matters more than more calls. INFERRED.
- The GRA finding "selective access, not topology" is a warning for a graph-reasoning paper: we need a graph-free control (same tools minus edges) to attribute any gain to structure. CNY's degree-preserving rewire is the cleanest such control and is cheap to reproduce on call graphs. INFERRED.
- Community-detection summaries (GraphRAG style) at 1k–5k nodes are cheap to *compute* (Louvain/Leiden on 6k edges is milliseconds), but LLM-written summaries per community would cost many calls per repo. A non-LLM variant (top-degree/central symbols + file names per community) may give most of the "map" value. INFERRED; no paper found that tests this on code.

- No paper found that reports call/token cost of ToG/ToG-2 under a fixed call budget in the units we need. Original ToG cost numbers were not re-checked this session.
- LightRAG peer-review status not verified.
- No controlled study found of serialised-graph accuracy vs graph size in the 1k–5k node range specifically (the "Talk like a graph" line is older and was not re-checked).

## Q2. Does structural/process supervision (rewarding the path, not only the answer) improve generalisation? Which reward designs are verifiable without humans?

### Takeaway
Yes, consistently but modestly, and with caveats. Across five independent 2026 works (EoG [ICLR'26], PathRouter, GraphCert, VulAgentRL, CNY), adding a path/evidence term on top of the outcome reward improves in-distribution scores by ~3–9 points and improves cross-dataset/OOD retention. All of the best designs are machine-verifiable: gold-path triple recall, evidence-overlap F1, executable rubric matching, exact node-ID F1 against a graph, and hindsight self-distillation. Two caveats recur: (i) process terms that reward *efficiency* (fewer revisits) can cause premature stopping and hurt transfer (GraphEcho-PAPT), and (ii) SFT warm start is structurally required before GRPO in every tool-use paper.

### Cited Findings
- **Explore-on-Graph (ICLR 2026)** uses reward `R = F1_outcome + α·R_path`. R_path is the fraction of ground-truth reasoning triples articulated in the think trace. Gold paths come from BFS between topic and answer entities within a hop limit, then LLM-verified to drop "topologically connected but logically irrelevant spurious paths". Removing the path reward drops CWQ F1 73.9 → 70.8, and path reward yields shorter outputs. EoG had higher OOD/IID ratios than its SFT-only variant. Both too-low and too-high α hurt. VERIFIED. — [Explore-on-Graph, arXiv 2602.21728](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.21728)
- **PathRouter** classifies each rollout by answer correctness C and evidence-path overlap P into faithful success (C↑P↑), shortcut (C↑P↓), evidence-retrieved (C↓P↑) and joint failure. It scales GRPO advantages per route (down-weights shortcuts, preserves evidence-retrieved failures). It adds reward shaping: an exploration bonus, a **lazy penalty for single-turn stops with insufficient evidence**, a timeout penalty and a redundancy penalty. It also applies selective teacher KL from a frozen same-size model *given the gold evidence*, only on evidence-poor trajectories and only on reasoning/query tokens (answer tokens masked). VERIFIED. — [PathRouter, arXiv 2606.16409](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16409)
- PathRouter results: 7B avg F1 57.82 → 62.74 vs Graph-R1; gains largest on multi-hop (HotpotQA +7.4 F1, MuSiQue +8.2 F1). Cross-dataset retention averages 95.7% (vs Graph-R1 85.8%, Search-R1 70.6%). Removing the exploration bonus or lazy penalty collapsed retrieval to ~1 turn. Online self-distillation was worse than a frozen teacher. A 7B teacher for a 1.5B student "sharply degraded" performance. VERIFIED. — [PathRouter, 2606.16409](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16409)
- **GraphCert** has a compact Qwen3-4B "Quizzer" generate QA pairs plus marked evidence, executes and certifies them, and canonicalises the evidence into executable rubrics (Cypher). The Solver's reward is answer correctness plus rubric alignment, computed as a weighted pair score over shape, contract, ops and related comparisons. Certification rejects ~36% of self-generated candidates; hard failures include schema-direction errors (348 of 3,126 in Healthcare) and answer–evidence inconsistencies. VERIFIED. — [GraphCert, arXiv 2609.38798](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38798)
- GraphCert ablation (F1): full 0.722/0.681 (Healthcare/Literature); without certification 0.612/0.624; without the evidence reward 0.566/0.654. Trained 4B beats DeepSeek-V3.2-based GraphCounselor/GraphCoT on GRBench. VERIFIED. — [GraphCert, 2609.38798](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38798)
- **VulAgentRL ("Graph Is the Verifier")** is a code-graph example. Composite reward `0.6·verdict + 0.2·F1(cited CPG node IDs vs teacher evidence set) + 0.2·CWE-tier`. Adding the grounding term gave the largest gain: pair-correct 0.289 → 0.378 (+8.9), accuracy +4.7. RL cut tool calls 7.6 → 4.3 vs SFT. A verdict-only reward led to "evidence-free degeneration" (over-predicting vulnerable). Cold-start GRPO never learned to call tools (0 calls/rollout), while SFT-initialised runs reached 16.6 turns. SFT data was filtered to trajectories whose cited node IDs exist and lie within 2 hops of the patched function. VERIFIED. — [Graph Is the Verifier, arXiv 2607.26656](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26656)
- **CNY** uses destination-conditioned on-policy self-distillation. After a walk reveals node X, the same policy re-scores the walk tokens with a short topic recap of X. The log-prob difference is added as per-action credit (β=0.03). Training reward was similar with and without it, but held-out accuracy was "markedly higher" with OPSD. A β-sweep on Qwen3-4B monotonically improved held-out WikiCS (+5.6) and Cora (+7.0). It needs no labels or judges and costs ~24% extra compute. VERIFIED. — [Call Neighbours Yourself, 2608.29588](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29588)
- **GraphEcho PAPT** (Qwen3-4B, LoRA r16, GRPO): `R = verdict + 0.4·count + 0.4·coverage − 0.4·echo − 0.05·cost`. Synthetic accuracy +41.5 pp and echo-walk rate −36.9 pp, but source recall fell, walks shortened to ~2, and SciFact transfer accuracy fell 3.6 pp. The authors conclude "an agent can learn to stop repeating itself while overlooking information it needs". VERIFIED. — [GraphEcho, arXiv 2609.17695](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17695)
- **SOG-R1** shows a deterministic outcome reward with no judge, plus a multiplicative efficiency factor among correct rollouts only, is sufficient after strong SFT. RL uplift over SFT is larger with less data (+7.8 EM at 2,700 questions vs +2.4 at 8,700). VERIFIED. — [SOG-R1, 2607.18481](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.18481)
- **A²Agent** (code localisation, SWE-bench) uses per-turn rewards for *discovery* and *commitment* of gold code regions, with action-level advantage grouping turns that share exploration context. It reports +1.58% F1 (Verified) and +8.55% (Pro) over SOTA, and a 4B model beats baselines up to 8× larger. It explicitly targets agents that "discover correct code regions during exploration but fail to commit them". VERIFIED (abstract + section grep). — [A²Agent, arXiv 2608.29831](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29831)

**Catalogue of human-free, verifiable process rewards seen (all VERIFIED as described above):**
1. Gold-path triple recall in the trace (EoG). Gold paths come from BFS + LLM filter, so it is semi-automatic.
2. Evidence-overlap F1 between retrieved items and gold supporting items, plus a 2×2 route-conditioned advantage (PathRouter).
3. Executable-rubric alignment: canonicalised graph queries compared structurally (GraphCert).
4. Exact node-ID F1 of cited evidence against a graph-derived set, plus a 2-hop validity filter on SFT data (VulAgentRL).
5. Hindsight self-distillation credit on walk tokens (CNY). No gold needed.
6. Behavioural shaping: lazy-stop penalty, redundancy/echo penalty, timeout penalty, turn-efficiency factor (PathRouter, GraphEcho, SOG-R1).
7. Per-turn gold-region discovery/commit reward (A²Agent, code).

### Inferences
- The project's T1 reward (chain validity vs a direction-repaired graph + gold hit) is in the same family as #3/#4. The closest code precedent is **VulAgentRL** (cited-node-ID F1 against a CPG, on a 7B model with SFT→GRPO). The paper must cite it and draw a clear line: VulAgentRL verifies *cited evidence nodes* for vulnerability verdicts. It does not verify *edge-by-edge path validity* or name-invariance, and it is not SWE issue localisation. INFERRED.
- A²Agent already rewards gold-region discovery per turn in SWE localisation, so "dense localisation rewards" alone is not novel. The open space is rewarding **structural faithfulness of the claimed path** (each hop is a real, correctly directed edge) and **invariance under renaming**. INFERRED.
- PathRouter's 2×2 taxonomy maps cleanly onto code: C = gold file/function hit, P = claimed chain overlaps a real graph path to gold. "Shortcut success" (C↑P↓) is exactly "found the fix by name match without a structural path", the lexical-shortcut behaviour documented by Schrödinger's Repo / Face/Off in HQ. Down-weighting C↑P↓ is a cheap, testable intervention. INFERRED.
- Given the incomplete-graph theorem (Q4/Q3), the path reward should give *partial* credit for hops that are absent from the graph but recoverable from source text (e.g., `B(` appears in A's body). Unsupported is not the same as contradicted. INFERRED.
- Under LoRA-only on a 31B model with $50, full GRPO on the 31B is likely infeasible. The verifiable rewards can instead be used as **rejection-sampling filters for SFT** (as VulAgentRL and SOG-R1 do for their SFT data) on the 31B, with GRPO ablations on a small Gemma. INFERRED.

### Gaps
- No paper found that isolates path reward vs outcome reward under **isomorphic renaming** outside GRAIN (already covered).
- None of the process-reward papers report results at ≥30B with LoRA only. Most are 1.5B–14B. Transfer of the effect sizes to 31B is unknown.
- EoG's OOD gains were read from a figure description, not exact numbers.

## Q3. Known failure modes (premature stop, hallucinated edges, hub shortcuts, lexical shortcuts, redundancy)

### Takeaway
The 2026 literature names and measures six recurring failures of LLM graph agents:
1. Premature/lazy stopping, plus its mirror image: discovered-but-not-committed.
2. Shortcut success: a correct answer from parametric memory or names without traversing evidence.
3. Echo walks: revisiting redundant paths to the same evidence, mistaken for corroboration.
4. Dropped frontier entities when expanding one-at-a-time.
5. Hallucinated or wrong-direction edges in self-generated supervision and in linearised contexts.
6. Tool-call format failures that silently erase the agentic advantage.

Hub shortcuts specifically are under-studied in general graph work. The best evidence that they matter is our own A2 finding (hub-free BFS increases unreachability).

### Cited Findings
- **Premature/lazy stop.** Without PathRouter's exploration bonus or lazy penalty, agents collapse to ~1 retrieval turn. VERIFIED. — [PathRouter](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16409). GraphEcho shows a stopping rule based on balance of *acquired* evidence can't tell "graph exhausted" from "four sources unseen". Efficiency-trained PAPT stops after ~2 walks and visits about one-third of available origins. VERIFIED. — [GraphEcho](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17695)
- **Discovered but not committed.** Code localisation agents "often discover correct code regions during exploration but fail to commit them". VERIFIED. — [A²Agent](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29831). (GraphDancer's evidence-hit vs conversion split is the same phenomenon; already in project notes.)
- **Shortcut success / parametric fallback.** PathRouter defines C↑P↓ "shortcut failure": correct answers from parametric memory with low evidence overlap. Outcome-only RL reinforces it ("answer-path reward aliasing"). VERIFIED. — [PathRouter](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.16409). Mechanistically, linearised graphs trigger FFN grounding failure and reversion to parametric memory. VERIFIED. — [2605.26362](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.26362). SOG-R1 bans parametric fallback by construction (all facts via live Search). EoG/KG-Hopper allow it. VERIFIED. — [SOG-R1](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.18481)
- **Lexical/structural-cue shortcuts in attention.** Attention concentrates on "shortcut-like structural cues rather than distributing across the full context". VERIFIED. — [2605.26362](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.26362)
- **Echo walks / false corroboration.** Redundant supporting paths raise echo-walk rates in all four frozen agents (+5.8 to +38.8 pp). Qwen3-8B overcounts sources by 4 at 1-source/5-path. GPT-5-mini accuracy drops 61.9 pp under redundant support. Cheap preprocessing ("provenance collapse": group equivalent paths) nearly eliminates redundant-path sensitivity while keeping sensitivity to genuinely distinct sources. VERIFIED. — [GraphEcho](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.17695)
- **Dropped frontier.** Agents issue calls for a subset of the entities at a hop and lose answer paths. Fixed by a batched-entity tool. VERIFIED. — [SOG-R1](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.18481)
- **Hallucinated / wrong-direction edges in generated supervision.** In GraphCert's self-generated candidates, 348 of 3,126 (Healthcare) were schema-direction errors and 314 of 3,096 (Literature) were answer–evidence inconsistencies. Unfiltered training drops F1 0.722 → 0.612. VERIFIED. — [GraphCert](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.38798). VulAgentRL rejects teacher trajectories citing nonexistent node IDs or nodes beyond 2 hops of the patched function. VERIFIED. — [Graph Is the Verifier](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26656)
- **Evidence-free degeneration.** A verdict-only reward makes the code agent exploit the class prior: it labels both the vulnerable and the patched version vulnerable in 31.7% of pairs. VERIFIED. — [Graph Is the Verifier](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26656)
- **Tool unreliability.** The agentic advantage reverses when tool-call failure exceeds a few percent. VERIFIED. — [GRA](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.15834)
- **Incomplete graph.** Absence of an edge is not falsity. Hard grounding provably can't separate true-unobserved from false-unsupported. The authors recommend soft (Gibbs/KL) grounding with finite slack, direction-sensitive energies, and abstain/retrieve-more when support is low. VERIFIED. — [Grounding LLM Reasoning under Incomplete Graph Evidence](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2606.30247). A hybrid embedding+LLM KGQA method targets traversal "under incompleteness" by not relying only on existing edges (abstract only). — [Explore-on-Graph (NYUAD), 2609.39786](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.39786)
- **CNY residual errors** after learning to walk: adjacent-class interference 53.5%, misleading same-class neighbour 39.4%, reasoning drift 6.7%. Errors come from plausible-but-non-discriminative evidence, not search failure. VERIFIED. — [CNY](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.29588)

### Inferences
- **Hub shortcuts.** I found no 2025–26 general-graph paper that isolates hub-node shortcuts as a failure mode of LLM agents. GraphEcho's redundancy is the closest analogue: many paths via a hub look like corroboration. In call graphs, hubs (logging, `__init__`, utility functions, test fixtures) create short spurious paths, which matches our A2 hub-free BFS finding. Treat this as an open, citable gap. INFERRED.
- Provenance collapse transfers almost one-to-one to code: collapse class→method containment edges and duplicate/self-loop/parallel edges (2,592 self-loops and 94 duplicates in our graphs, per HQ) into single "evidence origins" before showing the agent its neighbourhood. INFERRED.
- Our undirected "calls" graph produces the same direction errors GraphCert counts as schema-direction failures. Our source-text direction repair (86–90%) plays the role of GraphCert's execution certification. INFERRED.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found of hub-degree bias in LLM graph navigation (general or code).
- No paper measured lexical-shortcut rate on KGs under entity renaming apart from GRAIN (covered) and GraphEcho's fictional entities (which control for, rather than measure, the effect).

## Q4. Which ideas have NOT yet been applied to code graphs / SWE agents? (with search log)

### Takeaway
Of the general-graph ideas above, several appear unapplied to code call graphs / SWE localisation as of 8 Oct 2026 (within the limits of the searches below):
1. Route-conditioned (C×P) advantage scaling against shortcut success.
2. Provenance/echo-aware exploration and provenance collapse.
3. Destination-conditioned hindsight credit for neighbour walks (CNY-OPSD).
4. Soft, evidence-relative path grounding that tolerates missing edges.
5. Batched, direction-filtered, paged neighbour tools learned via SFT→RL with an efficiency factor.
6. Degree-preserving-rewire controls to prove structure use.

Partially taken: evidence-node-ID grounding rewards on a code graph (VulAgentRL, vulnerability detection), and per-turn gold-discovery/commit rewards in SWE localisation (A²Agent). Not found anywhere: edge-by-edge *path-validity* reward under *name remapping* for SWE localisation (the project's T1).

A²Agent gives per-turn discovery/commit rewards over a toolset including `TraverseGraph` for SWE-bench localisation.
